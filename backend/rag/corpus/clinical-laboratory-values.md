# Clinical Laboratory Values

Laboratory values are interpreted in the context of the individual
patient, and reference ranges vary modestly between laboratories.
The values below are the commonly used adult reference intervals for
the markers relevant to diabetes, hypertension, chronic kidney disease,
and sepsis.

## Glucose and glycaemic control

Fasting plasma glucose: 70-99 mg/dL (3.9-5.5 mmol/L). Impaired fasting
glucose: 100-125 mg/dL. Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher. Random plasma
glucose above 200 mg/dL with symptoms is diagnostic of diabetes.
Glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c): 4-5.6% is normal; 5.7-6.4% indicates
prediabetes; 6.5% or higher indicates diabetes. In most adults with
diabetes the treatment target is below 7.0%.

## Lipids

Total cholesterol: desirable below 200 mg/dL. LDL cholesterol:
optimal below 100 mg/dL, below 70 mg/dL in high-risk patients. HDL
cholesterol: 40 mg/dL or higher in men and 50 mg/dL or higher in women.
Triglycerides: normal below 150 mg/dL; 150-499 mg/dL is borderline
high/high; 500 mg/dL or higher is very high and warrants treatment.

## Renal function

Serum creatinine: approximately 0.6-1.2 mg/dL in adults, higher in men
than women and dependent on muscle mass. Estimated glomerular filtration
rate (eGFR): 90 mL/min/1.73m2 or higher is normal; 60-89 mild decrease;
45-59 mild-to-moderate; 30-44 moderate-to-severe; 15-29 severe; below 15
kidney failure. Urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio: normal below
30 mg/g; 30-300 mg/g moderately increased; above 300 mg/g severely
increased. Serum potassium: 3.5-5.0 mmol/L; both hypo- and
hyperkalaemia are dangerous.

## Inflammation and infection

C-reactive protein: normally below 3 mg/L; markedly elevated in
bacterial infection and sepsis. Lactate: normal below 2 mmol/L;
a lactate above 2 mmol/L with a vasopressor requirement defines septic
shock, and rising lactate indicates inadequate tissue perfusion.
White blood cell count: 4.0-11.0 x 10^9/L, with neutrophilia suggesting
bacterial infection and lymphocytosis a viral aetiology. Procalcitonin
is an adjunctive biomarker for bacterial infection.

## Interpretation caution

A single abnormal value must be confirmed and interpreted with the
clinical picture. Creatinine-based eGFR is unreliable in patients with
extremes of muscle mass. HbA1c is falsely low in haemolytic anaemia and
pregnancy, and falsely high in iron-deficiency anaemia and chronic
kidney disease. Renal function and electrolyte derangements can develop
rapidly in sepsis and require repeated measurement.
