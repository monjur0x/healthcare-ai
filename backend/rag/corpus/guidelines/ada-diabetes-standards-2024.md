# American Diabetes Association Standards of Care in Diabetes 2024

## Classification and Diagnosis

### Diabetes Diagnostic Criteria
| Test | Prediabetes | Diabetes |
|------|------------|----------|
| FPG | 100-125 mg/dL | ≥ 126 mg/dL |
| 2-hr OGTT | 140-199 mg/dL | ≥ 200 mg/dL |
| HbA1c | 5.7-6.4% | ≥ 6.5% |
| Random glucose with symptoms | - | ≥ 200 mg/dL |

### Screening Recommendations
- All adults beginning at age 35 years
- Overweight/obese adults with ≥ 1 risk factor at any age
- Repeat every 3 years if normal; annually if prediabetes

### Categories of Increased Risk (Prediabetes)
- Impaired fasting glucose: FPG 100-125 mg/dL
- Impaired glucose tolerance: 2-hr PG 140-199 mg/dL
- HbA1c: 5.7-6.4%

## Comprehensive Medical Evaluation

### At Each Visit:
1. Blood pressure measurement
2. Weight/BMI assessment
3. Foot inspection
4. Medication review and adherence

### Annually:
1. Dilated comprehensive eye exam
2. Comprehensive foot exam with monofilament testing
3. uACR and eGFR (kidney disease screening)
4. Lipid panel
5. Dental examination referral

### Every 2 Years:
- BMD screening for T1DM > 65 or postmenopausal

## Glycemic Targets

### General Targets:
- **HbA1c**: < 7.0% for most nonpregnant adults
- **Preprandial glucose**: 80-130 mg/dL
- **Peak postprandial**: < 180 mg/dL (1-2 hours after meal)
- **Time in range** (>70-180): > 70%

### Individualized Targets:
| Patient Profile | Suggested A1c |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Young, healthy, short duration | < 6.5% |
| Most adults | < 7.0% |
| Limited life expectancy, hypoglycemia risk | < 8.0% |
| Very complex/poor health | Avoid symptomatic hyperglycemia |

## Pharmacologic Treatment Algorithm (2024 Update)

### First-line Therapy Depends on Comorbidities:

#### For patients with ASCVD, high risk of ASCVD, or established kidney disease:
1. GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven CVD benefit OR
2. SGLT2 inhibitor with proven CVD/kidney benefit
   - If A1c above target despite these agents, continue them + add other agents as needed

#### For patients with heart failure:
1. SGLT2 inhibitor with proven benefit (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin)
   - Independent of A1c target

#### For patients with CKD:
1. SGLT2 inhibitor (eGFR ≥ 20) preferred
2. Consider finerenone for CKD with albuminuria
3. GLP-1 RA if needed for further glycemic control

#### For patients without comorbidities:
1. Metformin remains first-line for most patients
2. Consider GLP-1 RA or dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist if weight management priority

### Combination Therapy Approach
- Early combination therapy may be beneficial for newly diagnosed T2DM with A1c ≥ 1.5% above target
- Triple therapy if dual therapy inadequate after 3 months

### Insulin Therapy
- Basal insulin: start 10 units/day or 0.1-0.2 units/kg/day
- Titrate by 2 units every 3 days to fasting target
- Add prandial insulin when basal dose > 0.5 units/kg without control
- Consider GLP-1 RA before intensifying to prandial insulin

## Cardiovascular Risk Management

### Blood Pressure
- Target: < 130/80 mm Hg if safely attained
- ACEi or ARB recommended for diabetes with hypertension
- Multiple medications often required (average 3+)

### Lipids
| Age/Disease Status | Target LDL |
|--------------------|-----------|
| 40-75 years, no ASCVD, no risk factors | Moderate-intensity statin |
| 40-75 years with ASCVD risk factors | High-intensity statin |
| Any age with established ASCVD | High-intensity statin (LDL goal < 70) |
| Very high risk ASCVD | Max statin + ezetimibe ± PCSK9i (LDL < 55) |

### Antiplatelet Therapy
- Aspirin 75-162 mg/day for secondary prevention
- Primary prevention: individualize based on ASCVD risk vs bleeding risk

## Kidney Disease Management
- Annual screening: uACR + eGFR
- Optimize BP control (< 130/80)
- Use ACEi/ARB when albuminuria present even without hypertension
- SGLT2 inhibitors reduce CKD progression regardless of glycemic response
- Finerenone (nonsteroidal MRA) reduces CKD progression in T2DM

## Retinopathy
- Optimize glycemic and BP control
- Anti-VEGF therapy for center-involved diabetic macular edema
- Pan-retinal photocoagulation for high-risk proliferative retinopathy

## Neuropathy
- Screen with monofilament testing + one of: vibration, pinprick, ankle reflexes
- Pregabalin, duloxetine, or gabapentin for painful neuropathy
- Comprehensive foot care education

## Special Populations

### Older Adults
- Consider cognitive impairment screening
- Simplify regimens when appropriate
- Avoid hypoglycemia (higher risk)

### Hospitalized Patients
- Insulin is preferred method for glycemic management
- Target 140-180 mg/dL for most critically ill
- Do not use sliding scale alone in non-critically ill

## Key Clinical Trials
- **EMPA-REG OUTCOME** (2015): Empagliflozin reduced CV death by 38%
- **LEADER** (2016): Liraglutide reduced CV death by 22%
- **SUSTAIN-6** (2017): Semaglutide reduced MACE by 26%
- **DECLARE-TIMI 58** (2019): Dapagliflozin reduced hospitalization for HF
- **REWIND** (2019): Dulaglutide reduced MACE by 12%
- **FLOW** (2024): Semaglutide reduces kidney events by 24%
