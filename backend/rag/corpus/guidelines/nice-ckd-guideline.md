# NICE Guideline NG203: Chronic Kidney Disease - Assessment and Management

## Summary

This guideline covers care and treatment for people with, or at risk of developing, chronic kidney disease (CKD). It aims to prevent or delay progression, reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease, and manage complications.

## Key Recommendations

### 1. Investigation and Monitoring

**eGFR Calculation:**
- Use serum creatinine and CKD-EPI equation to estimate GFR
- Report eGFR alongside creatinine results
- Confirm eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m² on 2 occasions separated by > 90 days before diagnosing CKD

**Albuminuria:**
- Use uACR to detect and quantify proteinuria
- First morning urine sample preferred
- Confirm positive uACR on repeat sample within 3 months

**Frequency of Monitoring:**
| Condition | eGFR monitoring | uACR monitoring |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| No CKD, risk factors | Every 2-5 years | Not routine |
| CKD G1-G3a | Every 6-12 months | Every 6-12 months |
| CKD G3b-G4 | Every 3-6 months | Every 3-6 months |
| CKD G5 | Every 1-3 months | Monthly |

### 2. Information and Education
- Explain that most people with CKD do not progress to kidney failure
- Discuss modifiable risk factors (smoking, weight, BP)
- Provide written information about CKD

### 3. Blood Pressure Management

**Targets:**
| Patient Group | Clinic SBP Target |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Age < 80 with CKD + ACR ≥ 30 mg/mmol | 120-129 mm Hg |
| Age < 80 without albuminuria | 140 mm Hg or below |
| Age ≥ 80 with CKD | Same as above if tolerated |

**Drug Treatment:**
- ACEi or ARB first-line for hypertension in CKD with ACR ≥ 30 mg/mmol
- Do not offer ACEi/ARB for primary prevention of CKD without albuminuria
- Monitor serum potassium and creatinine after initiation
- Accept up to 30% rise in creatinine from baseline when starting RAAS inhibitor

### 4. Renin-Angiotensin System Antagonists

**Indications for ACEi/ARB:**
- Hypertension with CKD and ACR ≥ 30 mg/mmol (without diabetes)
- Diabetes with ACR ≥ 3 mg/mmol (microalbuminuria)

**Monitoring After Starting ACEi/ARB:**
- Check serum potassium and creatinine within 1-2 weeks
- If creatinine rises > 30% from baseline:
  - Check for bilateral renal artery stenosis
  - Consider reducing dose or stopping
- Hyperkalemia: address causes first; consider dose reduction or potassium binder

### 5. SGLT2 Inhibitors (2021 Update)

Offer SGLT2 inhibitors to patients with CKD who have:
- Type 2 diabetes AND eGFR ≥ 25 mL/min/1.73m²
- OR type 2 diabetes with ACR ≥ 200 mg/g

Evidence: DAPA-CKD and EMPA-KIDNEY trials show kidney protection independent of glucose lowering.

### 6. Assessment of Cardiovascular Risk
- People with CKD are at high risk of CVD
- Offer statins for primary prevention of CVD to all people with CKD
- Do not routinely assess lipid profile before starting statins in CKD

### 7. Anaemia in CKD

**Investigate anaemia if:**
- Hb < 110 g/L in adults
- Hb < 100 g/L in children

**Management:**
1. Correct iron deficiency first (oral or IV iron)
2. ESA therapy if Hb remains < 100 g/L despite iron repletion
3. Target Hb: 100-115 g/L (do not target normal range)
4. Investigate other causes of anaemia (B12, folate, GI bleeding)

### 8. Mineral Bone Disease
- Monitor calcium, phosphate, PTH from CKD G3a onwards
- Dietary phosphate restriction
- Phosphate binders when dietary measures insufficient
- Vitamin D supplementation as needed

### 9. Acidosis
- Oral sodium bicarbonate if serum bicarbonate < 22 mmol/L
- May slow progression of CKD

### 10. Referral Criteria to Specialist Kidney Services

Refer when any of:
- eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m² (G4-G5)
- ACR ≥ 70 mg/mmol (confirmed)
- eGFR decline ≥ 5 mL/min/1.73m² in 1 year
- Hypertension poorly controlled despite 4+ antihypertensive drugs
- Known/suspected genetic cause of CKD
- Suspected renal artery stenosis

Urgent referral:
- eGFR < 15
- Rapidly declining eGFR
- Refractory hyperkalemia

## Self-Care and Lifestyle
1. Keep salt intake low (< 6 g/day sodium chloride)
2. Ensure adequate fluid intake (> 1.5L/day unless contraindicated)
3. Exercise regularly
4. Stop smoking
5. Maintain healthy weight
6. Limit alcohol
7. Avoid NSAIDs
8. Annual influenza vaccination
9. Pneumococcal vaccination

## Medication Review
Review all medications at least annually in CKD:
- Avoid NSAIDs where possible
- Adjust dosing for renally cleared drugs
- Consider drug-drug interactions
- Review contrast exposure risk
