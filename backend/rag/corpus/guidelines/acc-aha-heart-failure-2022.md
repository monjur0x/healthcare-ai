# ACC/AHA/HFSA 2022 Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of Heart Failure

## Classification

### New Universal Definition of Heart Failure (2022)
HF is a clinical syndrome with symptoms and/or signs caused by a structural and/or functional cardiac abnormality and corroborated by elevated natriuretic peptide levels and/or objective evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion.

### Stages of HF (Prevention-focused):
- **Stage A**: At risk for HF (hypertension, diabetes, obesity, cardiotoxins) without current/prior symptoms
- **Stage B**: Pre-HF (structural heart disease or elevated biomarkers without symptoms)
- **Stage C**: Current or previous symptomatic HF
- **Stage D**: Advanced refractory HF

### NYHA Functional Classes:
- **Class I**: No limitation of physical activity
- **Class II**: Slight limitation; comfortable at rest
- **Class III**: Marked limitation; less than ordinary activity causes symptoms
- **Class IV**: Symptoms at rest or with minimal exertion

## EF-Based Classification:
- **HFrEF**: LVEF ≤ 40%
- **HFmrEF**: LVEF 41-49%
- **HFpEF**: LVEF ≥ 50% (with evidence of structural/functional abnormalities)

## Prevention (Stage A)

### Risk Factor Management
1. **Hypertension**: Target SBP < 130 mm Hg to prevent HF
2. **Diabetes**: SGLT2 inhibitors reduce HF hospitalization independent of glycemic effect
3. **Obesity**: Weight loss reduces incident HF
4. **Smoking cessation**
5. **Excessive alcohol use**: Reduce or abstain

## Diagnosis and Evaluation

### Biomarkers
- BNP > 50 pg/mL or NT-proBNP > 125 pg/mL suggests HF in dyspneic non-acute patients
- In acute settings: BNP > 300 pg/mL or NT-proBNP > 900 pg/mL

### Echocardiography
- TTE recommended for all patients with suspected HF
- Assess: LVEF, chamber sizes, wall thickness, valve function, diastolic function

## Treatment Guidelines by Stage

### Stage C HFrEF: Four Foundational Therapies (GDMT)

#### Pillar 1: Evidence-based beta-blockers
- Carvedilol, Metoprolol succinate (XL), Bisoprolol
- Start low, titrate to target dose over weeks
- Contraindications: decompensated HF, bradycardia, severe asthma

#### Pillar 2: RAAS Inhibition
| Agent | Starting Dose | Target Dose |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Sacubitril/Valsartan (ARNI) | 24/26 mg BID | 97/103 mg BID |
| Lisinopril | 2.5-5 mg daily | 20-40 mg daily |
| Losartan | 25-50 mg daily | 150 mg daily |

ARNI preferred over ACEi/ARB as it showed superior outcomes in PARADIGM-HF

#### Pillar 3: Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA)
- Spironolactone 12.5-50 mg daily
- Eplerenone 25-50 mg daily
- Monitor potassium (hold if K+ > 5.0 mEq/L)
- RALES trial: 30% reduction in mortality

#### Pillar 4: SGLT2 Inhibitors
- Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily
- Empagliflozin 10 mg daily
- Benefit independent of diabetes status
- DAPA-HF: 26% reduction in CV death or worsening HF
- EMPEROR-Reduced: 25% reduction in primary outcome

### Additional Therapies:
- Loop diuretics for volume management (furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide)
- Hydralazine + Isosorbide dinitrate for Black patients with NYHA III-IV
- Ivabradine for HR ≥ 70 despite max beta-blocker

### Device Therapy
| Intervention | Indication | Key Trial |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| ICD | LVEF ≤ 35%, NYHA II-III, expected survival > 1 year | SCD-HeFT |
| CRT | LVEF ≤ 35%, LBBB QRS ≥ 150 ms, NYHA II-IV | CARE-HF |
| LVAD | Bridge-to-transplant or destination therapy | REMATCH |

### Sequence of GDMT Initiation (2022 Update)
All four foundational therapies should be initiated rapidly (within 4 weeks) rather than sequentially over months. Low doses can be used initially with rapid up-titration.

## HFpEF Treatment (2022 Major Update)

### First-line for HFpEF:
1. **SGLT2 inhibitors** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin)
   - EMPEROR-Preserved: 21% reduction in CV death or hospitalization
   - DELIVER: 18% reduction

2. **Diuretics** for congestion relief

### Additional considerations for HFpEF:
- MRA (TOPCAT: benefit in some subgroups)
- Treat underlying conditions (HTN, AFib, obesity)
- Exercise training improves functional capacity

## Acute Decompensated Heart Failure

### Assessment:
1. Clinical congestion assessment (JVD, edema, rales, orthopnea)
2. Chest X-ray for pulmonary congestion
3. BNP/NT-proBNP
4. Identify precipitating cause (ischemia, arrhythmia, non-adherence, infection)

### Management:
1. **Oxygen** if SpO₂ < 90%
2. **Loop diuretics** - IV furosemide 40-80 mg (or equivalent); double home dose
3. **Vasodilators** (nitroglycerin, nitroprusside) if hypertensive
4. **Inotropes** (dobutamine, milrinone) for low-output states
5. **Ultrafiltration** for diuretic-resistant congestion

### Discharge Planning:
- Optimize oral GDMT before discharge when possible
- Follow-up within 7 days of discharge
- Medication reconciliation critical
- Daily weights education
- Sodium restriction (< 2g/day)

|-------|------|------------|--------|
| PARADIGM-HF | 2014 | Sacubitril/Valsartan vs Enalapril | 20% ↓ CV death/HF hospitalization |
