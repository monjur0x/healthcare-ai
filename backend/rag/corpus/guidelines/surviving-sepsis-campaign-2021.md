# Surviving Sepsis Campaign: International Guidelines for Management of Sepsis and Septic Shock 2021

## Definitions

### Sepsis-3 Criteria
Sepsis = life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.
- Clinically defined as **suspected/documented infection + increase in SOFA score ≥ 2**
- SOFA score change of ≥ 2 predicts mortality > 10%

### Septic Shock
Subset of sepsis with circulatory, cellular, and metabolic dysfunction:
- Requires vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mm Hg
- Serum lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation
- Hospital mortality > 40%

### Screening Tools
- **qSOFA**: Respiratory rate ≥ 22, altered mentation, SBP ≤ 100 mm Hg (score ≥ 2 suggests high risk)
- **NEWS** or **MEWS** recommended over qSOFA as single screening tool

## Hour-1 Bundle (Updated from 3-hour/6-hour bundles)

### Within 1 Hour of Recognition:
1. **Measure lactate level** (remeasure if initial lactate > 2 mmol/L)
2. **Obtain blood cultures** before antibiotics (do not delay antibiotics)
3. **Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics**
4. **Begin rapid crystalloid fluid** - 30 mL/kg for hypotension or lactate ≥ 4 mmol/L
5. **Apply vasopressors** if hypotensive during or after fluids to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mm Hg

## Antimicrobial Therapy

### Timing
- Administer IV antimicrobials within 1 hour for septic shock and probable sepsis
- For possible sepsis without shock: within 3 hours is acceptable if rapid evaluation possible
- Do NOT use procalcitonin alone to determine when to start antibiotics

### Empiric Broad-Spectrum Therapy
- Use one or more antimicrobials with activity against all likely pathogens
- Consider local antibiogram, source of infection, patient risk factors
- De-escalate when pathogen identified and sensitivities available
- Duration: typically 7 days for most sources; longer for Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia, fungal, deep tissue infections

### Source Control
- Implement source control ASAP or within 6-12 hours of diagnosis
- Examples: drain abscess, debride infected tissue, remove infected device

## Hemodynamic Management

### Fluid Resuscitation
- At least 30 mL/kg crystalloid IV within first 3 hours
- Prefer balanced crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's, Plasma-Lyte) over normal saline
- After initial resuscitation: guide additional fluids by dynamic measures
  - Passive leg raise
  - Fluid challenge with stroke volume variation
  - Pulse pressure variation
- Avoid fluid overload; reassess frequently

### Vasopressors
| First-line | Norepinephrine | Preferred over other agents |
|------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Second-line | Vasopressin | Add when norepinephrine dose 0.25-0.5 mcg/kg/min |
| Third-line | Epinephrine | Add when norepinephrine + vasopressin insufficient |
| Adjunct | Dobutamine | Add for low cardiac output with persistent hypoperfusion |

- Target MAP: 65 mm Hg (higher target 80-85 may be considered in chronic hypertension)
- Start vasopressors peripherally if central access not yet available (safely dilute)

### Corticosteroids
- IV hydrocortisone 200 mg/day for septic shock not responsive to adequate fluids + vasopressors
- Suggest IV hydrocortisone at 200 mg/day (50 mg q6h or continuous infusion)
- Taper when vasopressors no longer needed

## Ventilation

### ARDS Management
- Lung protective ventilation: TV 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau pressure < 30 cm H₂O
- Higher PEEP strategy for moderate-severe ARDS
- Prone positioning: 12-16 hours daily for PaO₂/FiO₂ < 150 mm Hg
- Neuromuscular blockers: consider short-term infusion if refractory hypoxemia

## Glucose Control
- Target blood glucose ≤ 180 mg/dL (10 mmol/L)
- Insulin protocol with regular monitoring

## Renal Replacement Therapy
- Consider CRRT or intermittent RRT based on availability and expertise
- No strong recommendation for timing (early vs. delayed) unless:
  - Urgent indications present (refractory hyperkalemia, severe acidosis, volume overload)

## DVT Prophylaxis
- LMWH preferred over unfractionated heparin
- Combine pharmacological and mechanical prophylaxis when available

## Stress Ulcer Prophylaxis
- PPI or H2 blocker for patients with risk factors for GI bleeding

## Nutrition
- Early enteral nutrition (within 24-48 hours) rather than early parenteral
- No need for aggressive caloric delivery in first week

## Key Evidence
- **PROCESS trial** (2014): Protocolized resuscitation did not improve outcomes vs usual care
- **ARISE trial** (2014): EGDT showed no benefit over usual care
- **PROMISE trial** (2015): Confirmed ARISE findings across more centers
- **ANDROMEDA-SHOCK** (2019): Peripheral perfusion-targeted resuscitation comparable to lactate-targeted
