# Coronary Heart Disease and Dyslipidaemia

Coronary heart disease (CHD) results from atherosclerotic narrowing of
the coronary arteries, manifesting as stable angina, acute coronary
syndromes, heart failure, arrhythmia, or sudden cardiac death.
Atherosclerosis is a chronic inflammatory process driven by the
accumulation of cholesterol-laden plaques in the arterial wall;
rupture or erosion of a vulnerable plaque triggers thrombus formation
and acute myocardial infarction.

## Risk factors

Major modifiable risk factors are cigarette smoking, hypertension,
elevated low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, low
high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, diabetes mellitus, obesity,
physical inactivity, and an unhealthy diet. Non-modifiable factors are
age, male sex, and family history of premature cardiovascular disease.
Chronic kidney disease and systemic inflammation also accelerate
atherogenesis. The absolute risk of a cardiovascular event is a
product of multiple factors acting together, which is why composite
risk calculators outperform any single factor.

## Lipids and targets

LDL cholesterol is the primary atherogenic particle and the principal
treatment target. Optimal LDL is below 100 mg/dL for most adults and
below 70 mg/dL (and often below 55 mg/dL) in those at very high risk,
such as patients with established atherosclerotic disease or diabetes
with target-organ damage. Triglycerides above 150 mg/dL and a low HDL
cholesterol are additional markers of elevated residual risk.
Statins are the first-line lipid-lowering therapy, with ezetimibe and
PCSK9 inhibitors as add-on options in patients not reaching target.

## Diagnosis

Stable angina is suspected with exertional chest discomfort relieved by
rest or nitrates. Acute coronary syndrome presents with prolonged chest
pain, diaphoresis, dyspnoea, or haemodynamic instability, and is
diagnosed by history, electrocardiogram changes, and elevated cardiac
troponin. Non-invasive testing includes exercise electrocardiography,
myocardial perfusion imaging, and coronary computed tomography
angiography; invasive coronary angiography is the gold standard for
anatomic assessment.

## Management

Management comprises lifestyle change, pharmacotherapy, and
revascularisation. Antiplatelet therapy with aspirin (and a P2Y12
inhibitor after acute events), statins to a target LDL, blood pressure
control, and glycaemic control in diabetics form the pharmacological
core. Beta-blockers and nitrates relieve angina; ACE inhibitors are
indicated after myocardial infarction and in left ventricular
dysfunction. Percutaneous coronary intervention and coronary artery
bypass grafting revascularise obstructive disease. Cardiac
rehabilitation and smoking cessation substantially reduce recurrence
and mortality.
