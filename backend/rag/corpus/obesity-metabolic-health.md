# Obesity, Nutrition, and Metabolic Health

Obesity is an abnormal or excessive accumulation of body fat that impairs
health. It is most commonly quantified by body mass index (BMI),
calculated as weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in
metres. A BMI of 18.5-24.9 kg/m2 is normal, 25-29.9 kg/m2 overweight,
30-34.9 kg/m2 class I obesity, 35-39.9 kg/m2 class II obesity, and
40 kg/m2 or higher class III (severe) obesity. Waist circumference and
waist-to-height ratio additionally capture central adiposity, which
carries disproportionate metabolic risk.

## Metabolic consequences

Excess adiposity drives insulin resistance and is a core contributor to
type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver
disease, obstructive sleep apnoea, and cardiovascular disease. The
metabolic syndrome is the clustering of central obesity, raised
triglycerides, reduced HDL cholesterol, raised blood pressure, and
impaired fasting glucose; it identifies individuals at high risk of both
diabetes and cardiovascular events. Weight loss of even 5-10% improves
glycaemia, blood pressure, and lipid profiles measurably.

## Nutritional principles

A healthy dietary pattern emphasises vegetables, fruit, whole grains,
legumes, nuts, fish, and unsaturated fats, while limiting added sugars,
refined carbohydrates, processed meats, and sodium. Glycaemic management
benefits from consistent carbohydrate intake and selection of
low-glycaemic-index foods. Sodium intake below 2300 mg per day supports
blood pressure control; in hypertensive patients a lower target of
1500 mg may be appropriate. Energy balance, not any single nutrient, is
the determinant of weight change.

## Physical activity

Adults should accumulate at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-
intensity aerobic activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus
resistance training twice weekly. Physical activity improves insulin
sensitivity, lowers blood pressure and triglycerides, raises HDL
cholesterol, and reduces all-cause and cardiovascular mortality
independent of weight loss.

## Weight management interventions

Weight loss interventions are stepped by BMI and comorbidity: lifestyle
counselling for all; structured behavioural programmes and pharmacotherapy
(glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists such as semaglutide) for BMI
of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related comorbidity; and
bariatric surgery for BMI of 40 or higher, or 35 or higher with serious
comorbidity, where lifestyle and medication have failed.
