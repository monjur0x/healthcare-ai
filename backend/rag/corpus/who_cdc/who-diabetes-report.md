# WHO Global Report on Diabetes: Key Findings and Recommendations

## Global Burden
- 422 million adults worldwide have diabetes (2014 data)
- Prevalence has nearly doubled since 1980
- 1.6 million deaths directly attributed to diabetes each year
- Diabetes was the seventh leading cause of death globally

## Types of Diabetes

### Type 1 Diabetes
- Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells
- Usually diagnosed in children/young adults
- Requires lifelong insulin therapy
- Cannot be prevented currently

### Type 2 Diabetes (90%+ of cases)
- Insulin resistance with progressive beta-cell failure
- Strongly associated with obesity and physical inactivity
- Potentially preventable through lifestyle modification

### Gestational Diabetes
- Hyperglycemia first recognized during pregnancy
- Increases risk of T2DM later in life
- Associated with increased perinatal complications

## WHO Recommendations

### Population-Level Prevention
1. Implement national diabetes prevention programs
2. Reduce sugar-sweetened beverage consumption
3. Promote physical activity across populations
4. Improve food environments (labeling, taxation)
5. Strengthen health systems for NCD management

### Individual-Level Management
1. Screening high-risk individuals
2. Lifestyle modification as first intervention
3. Metformin for those not achieving targets with lifestyle alone
4. Regular monitoring of HbA1c, BP, lipids, kidneys, eyes, feet

### Integrated Care Model
WHO recommends integrating diabetes management into primary healthcare:
- Task-sharing with community health workers
- Standardized protocols for management
- Essential medicines availability
- Referral pathways for complications

## Complications and Their Prevention

### Microvascular
- Diabetic retinopathy: leading cause of blindness in working-age adults
- Diabetic nephropathy: leading cause of ESRD worldwide
- Diabetic neuropathy: affects up to 50% of patients over lifetime

### Macrovascular
- Cardiovascular disease: leading cause of death in diabetes
- Stroke risk doubled compared to non-diabetics
- Peripheral arterial disease leading to amputation

## Economic Impact
- Global healthcare expenditure on diabetes exceeds USD 825 billion annually
- Lost productivity adds significantly to economic burden
- Low-income countries bear disproportionate impact relative to resources

## Monitoring Framework
WHO recommends tracking:
- Diabetes prevalence by age group
- HbA1c control rates in treated patients
- Complication rates (amputations, dialysis initiation)
- Mortality attributed to diabetes
