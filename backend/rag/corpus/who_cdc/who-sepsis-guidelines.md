# WHO Clinical Management of Sepsis: Guidelines and Recommendations

## Global Impact
Sepsis affects 48.9 million people worldwide annually, causing 11 million deaths (19.7% of all global deaths). It is a leading cause of hospital deaths.

## WHO Priority Actions for Sepsis

### 1. Early Recognition
- Implement systematic screening in all healthcare settings
- Use validated tools (NEWS, MEWS) rather than qSOFA alone
- Train healthcare workers on sepsis recognition
- Target high-risk populations: neonates, elderly, immunocompromised, post-surgical

### 2. Antimicrobial Stewardship
- Ensure access to quality-assured antimicrobials
- Balance early administration with stewardship principles
- De-escalate based on culture results
- Monitor local resistance patterns

### 3. Infection Prevention
- Hand hygiene compliance
- Surgical site infection bundles
- Catheter-associated infection prevention
- Ventilator-associated pneumonia prevention
- Antimicrobial resistance surveillance

## WHO Essential Medicines List for Sepsis
| Category | Medicines |
|----------|----------|
| Antibiotics | Ceftriaxone, Piperacillin/Tazobactam, Meropenem, Vancomycin |
| Vasopressors | Norepinephrine, Epinephrine, Dopamine, Vasopressin |
| Corticosteroids | Hydrocortisone |
| Fluids | Ringer's Lactate, Normal Saline, 5% Albumin |
| Other | Insulin, Heparin, PPI |

## Resource-Limited Setting Considerations
1. Prioritize lactate measurement where available
2. Blood cultures may not be available - do not delay antibiotics
3. Peripheral vasopressor administration acceptable with monitoring
4. Mechanical ventilation may not be available - optimize non-invasive support
5. Consider local antimicrobial susceptibility patterns

## Maternal Sepsis (WHO Specific Focus)
Maternal sepsis is a leading cause of maternal mortality:
- Definition: life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection during pregnancy, childbirth, or postpartum
- Screening: temperature > 38°C or < 36°C, HR > 90, RR > 20, WBC abnormal
- Common sources: endometritis, urinary tract infection, wound infection, mastitis
- Treatment: broad-spectrum antibiotics + source control + supportive care
- Prevention: clean delivery practices, timely treatment of infections during pregnancy

## Neonatal Sepsis
- Leading cause of neonatal death globally (up to 30% of neonatal deaths)
- Early-onset (< 72 hours): usually from maternal transmission
- Late-onset (> 72 hours): nosocomial or community-acquired
- Empiric therapy: Ampicillin + Gentamicin (WHO essential medicines list)
- Prevention: clean birth practices, kangaroo mother care, breastfeeding promotion

## Antimicrobial Resistance
WHO identifies antimicrobial-resistant pathogens as a critical threat to sepsis management:
- ESBL-producing Enterobacteriaceae require carbapenems
- Carbapenem-resistant organisms need combination therapy
- MRSA requires vancomycin or linezolid
- Local antibiogram essential for empiric selection

## Quality Improvement
- Implement sepsis bundles with audit and feedback
- Regular education of healthcare workers
- Multidisciplinary sepsis response teams
- Performance metrics: time to antibiotics, fluid resuscitation completion rate
