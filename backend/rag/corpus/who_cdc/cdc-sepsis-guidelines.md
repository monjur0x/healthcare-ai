# CDC Guidelines: Sepsis Prevention and Management

## What is Sepsis?
Sepsis is the body's extreme response to an infection. It is a life-threatening medical emergency that happens when an infection you already have triggers a chain reaction throughout your body.

## Key Statistics
- At least 1.7 million adults in America develop sepsis each year
- Nearly 270,000 Americans die from sepsis annually
- 1 in 3 patients who die in a hospital have sepsis

## Warning Signs and Symptoms
### Sepsis symptoms include:
- Confusion or disorientation
- Shortness of breath
- High heart rate or low blood pressure
- Fever, shivering, or feeling very cold
- Extreme pain or discomfort
- Clammy or sweaty skin

## Risk Factors
1. Age 65 years or older
2. Weakened immune system
3. Recent hospitalization or surgery
4. Chronic conditions (diabetes, lung disease, kidney disease, cancer)
5. Previous episode of sepsis
6. Infants younger than 1 year

## CDC Hospital Sepsis Program Core Elements

### 1. Leadership Commitment
Hospital leadership must prioritize sepsis recognition and treatment.

### 2. Accountability
Designate a sepsis program leader responsible for outcomes.

### 3. Early Recognition Protocol
Implement systematic screening:
- Use EHR-based screening alerts
- Screen all patients with infection + organ dysfunction
- Use qSOFA, NEWS, or SIRS criteria as initial screeners

### 4. Treatment Protocols
Implement sepsis bundles:
- Blood cultures before antibiotics
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour of recognition
- Fluid resuscitation (30 mL/kg crystalloid)
- Vasopressors if hypotensive after fluids
- Lactate measurement and remeasurement if > 2 mmol/L

### 5. Data Tracking
Monitor:
- Time to first antibiotic
- Bundle compliance rate
- Sepsis mortality rates
- Readmission rates after sepsis admission

### 6. Education
Regular training on sepsis recognition for all clinical staff.

## Preventing Infections That Can Lead to Sepsis
1. Vaccination: influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19
2. Hand hygiene compliance
3. Catheter-associated infection prevention bundles
4. Surgical site infection prevention
5. Appropriate antibiotic use to prevent resistance

## Recovery After Sepsis
Post-sepsis syndrome affects many survivors:
- Long-term cognitive impairment
- Physical disability
- PTSD-like symptoms
- Increased risk of future infections

Follow-up care should address these long-term complications.
