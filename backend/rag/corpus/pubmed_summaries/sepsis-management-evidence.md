# Sepsis Management: Clinical Trial Evidence and Best Practices

## Early Antibiotics and Mortality

Liu et al. (2017) analyzed 35,000 septic patients:
- Each hour delay in antibiotic administration increased mortality by 4-8%
- Patients receiving antibiotics within first hour had highest survival

Seymour et al. (2017) NEJM analyzed time-to-treatment in emergency department:
- Delay of each hour between presentation and antibiotic administration increased mortality odds

## Fluid Resuscitation Trials

### PROCESS (2014)
Protocolized early goal-directed therapy did not improve outcomes vs usual care in 1341 patients.
Challenged the universality of Rivers protocol approach.

### ARISE (2014)
Australasian EGDT study confirmed PROCESS findings in 1600 patients.
No difference in 90-day mortality between EGDT and usual care.

### Key Learning
Individualized care guided by clinical assessment may be as effective as strict protocols.
Early recognition and antibiotics matter more than specific hemodynamic targets.

## Vasopressor Selection

### VANISH (2016)
Compared vasopressin plus hydrocortisone vs norepinephrine plus placebo in septic shock.
No significant difference in kidney failure-free days.
Vasopressin group had less arrhythmia.

### VASST (2008)
Vasopressin did not reduce mortality vs norepinephrine as initial vasopressor.
But vasopressin appeared beneficial in less severe shock.

## Corticosteroids

### ADRENAL (2018)
Hydrocortisone did not reduce 90-day mortality but accelerated shock reversal.

### APROCCHSS (2018)
Hydrocortisone plus fludrocortisone reduced 90-day mortality by 14% in severe septic shock.

Current recommendation: consider hydrocortisone 200 mg/day when shock not responsive to fluids and vasopressors.

## Source Control

Single-center studies consistently show that inadequate source control doubles mortality in intra-abdominal sepsis.
Recommendation: implement source control within 6-12 hours of diagnosis when feasible.

## Lactate Clearance

ANDROMEDA-SHOCK (2019):
Peripheral perfusion-targeted resuscitation was comparable to lactate-targeted approach.
Capillary refill group had lower lactate levels and possibly better organ function.

## Procalcitonin for De-escalation

PRORATA trial: procalcitonin-guided antibiotic discontinuation reduced duration without worse outcomes.
Current SSC recommendation: use procalcitonin to SHORTEN antibiotic course but NOT to decide whether to START antibiotics.
